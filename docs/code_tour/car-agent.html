01Big picture

One car. It is the largest file after SODA because the traffic both controllers are measured on is only as good as this model. docs/unity/Unity_BASELINE.md is the reference for how it behaves; this page walks the code behind it.

  • Profile: each car draws Aggressive, Normal or Cautious at spawn, which fixes its speed, acceleration, braking, reaction time and amber behaviour.
  • Speed: every step it takes the lowest of several limits: free road, stopping at the line, the curve ahead, the car ahead and the comfortable time gap.
  • Signal: it reads its light every step and decides at amber whether it is too close to stop.
  • Path: turning lanes follow waypoints after the stop line; A2 changes lane into the shared lane before it.

Each frame runs the same short pipeline, in steps of at most 0.02 s:

03Sub-stepping: speed-independent motion

A batch runs at 10× speed. Without sub-steps a car at 14 m/s would move 2.3 m per frame, overshooting stop lines and lanes. With them, a car decides every 0.02 simulated seconds at any speed. The 1×/5×/10× check on one seed agreed within 3.2% for Adaptive and 0.45% for Fixed (SODA_BASELINE §8.6).

04Speed: free road and the stop line

Stopping at a red, closed loop

Until 24 Sep the stop was open loop. A car that arrived slower than approachSpeed braked from too far back and stopped short of the line, and could not move again until green. CB showed it worst: up to 27.5 m short for an all-Cautious queue. Now the car aims at the point and rolls up to it:

LaneBehaviourTest §4: the new head of a 12-car CB queue cut off by the amber now waits 0.00-0.04 m from its stop point. Before, it waited 10.6 m (all Normal), 18.8 m (mixed) and 27.5 m (all Cautious) back.

05Car following and the startup wave

This is the part that makes the simulated junction discharge at the real rate. On 15 Sep, Badin noticed queued cars "all pull away at once". HeadwayTest measured 0.72 s per car against the 2.31 s measured on site, which gave the model 3.2 times the real capacity. Two fixes followed:

  1. Startup wave. A stopped car starts its reaction timer when the car ahead moves, not when the light changes, so the queue leaves car by car.
  2. Time gap. The gap to the car ahead grows with speed: gap = minGap + desiredTimeHeadway × speed, applied as a speed cap.

What if: change desiredTimeHeadway

This is real output, not a sketch. Ten CarAgent instances were run outside Unity exactly as HeadwayTest runs them: stopped at a red, green at t = 0, the test's driver mix. The animation replays their recorded positions; the chart shows the mean headway at the stop line for every T from 0 to 3 s. At the code's 1.9 s the queue leaves 2.28 s apart, inside the 2.31 ± 0.15 band. Below about 1 s the headway stops falling, because reaction delays dominate.

Source: docs/code_tour/generator/measure_headway.cs, which compiles against the same build as the test harness. HeadwayTest itself prints 1.61 / 2.28 / 3.04 s for T = 1.0 / 1.9 / 2.8, matching these runs.

06The signal: push, poll and the amber decision

A light pushes every change to its registered cars, and each car also polls the light every step. Whichever arrives first gives the same result, so a lost registration cannot leave a car waiting forever.

Try it: who runs the amber

At the instant the light turns amber, a car carries on if it is within its braking distance × dilemma factor of the line. Once committed it ignores the red that follows. Ax reads the Bx light inverted and has no dilemma zone at all.

07Turning lanes

Ax, Bx, CB and CA drive straight to their stop line, then follow waypoints. Speed in the curve is capped at steering rate × turn radius = 55°/s × 7 m ≈ 6.7 m/s, and the car brakes for that in advance at its own decelRate.

08The A2 lane change

A2 is through traffic that spawns in lane A and moves into the outer lane it shares with Ax. It has to get there, because ROI_Ax is the only detector that sees it. The 24 Sep rebuild turned a blind swerve into a planned merge with gap acceptance:

LaneBehaviourTest §2 runs A2 against one Ax car: 3 light states × 9 driver pairs × 21 offsets = 567 runs. Overlapping bodies fell from 115 runs (worst 2.15 m, a full car width) to 0, and speed jumps from 292 to 0.

11Known issues and decisions

  • Fixed 15 SepQueue discharge 0.72 → 2.28 s/veh (startup wave + time gap). Every batch before v3 ran on the old model.Unity_BASELINE §7; SODA_BASELINE §8.15; HeadwayTest
  • Fixed 24 SepFree-road acceleration limited to the profile's rate; the comfort gap approached at decelRate; closed-loop red stop; A2 lane change rebuilt. Batch v5 is the first batch on this model. Where Ax traffic is heavy, A2 now really occupies the shared lane and waits for gaps, so lanes A and A2 slow down.Unity_BASELINE §6-7; LaneBehaviourTest
  • OpenA Cautious driver behind a hard-braking Aggressive one is held harder than its own profile allows (−6.2 to −9.4 m/s²), because maxSafeSpeed is the physical safety limit.Unity_BASELINE §5.1
  • OpenA car spawned at full speed behind a queue that reaches the spawn point brakes hard at once (off screen, within 25 m of a spawn point).Unity_BASELINE §5.2
  • OpenTurning lanes still turn at steerRate at any speed, so a car stopped mid-turn could rotate in place. Only the A2 change has the speed-limited heading. No visible case found.Unity_BASELINE §5.3
  • LimitationNo motorcycles, no PCU weighting; turning-lane headway is not measured separately, only lane B's.SODA_BASELINE L9, L11
