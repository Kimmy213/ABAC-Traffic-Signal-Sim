CarSpawner

Traffic452 linesRuntime

Releases cars into one lane, at Poisson-random or scripted times, with a seeded random stream of its own.

UsesCarAgent TrafficLight TestCaseLibrary
Used byIntersectionConfig ScenarioManager CustomTestCase ABACSceneBuilder
On this page

01Big picture

One spawner per lane, seven in the scene. Each releases cars at its lane's start point and hands every new car the settings of its lane. The most important design rule is that a spawner's random numbers never depend on traffic. Fixed and Adaptive can then be fed exactly the same cars, which is what makes the paired t-test valid.

  • Arrive: in Poisson mode at random gaps around meanArrivalInterval; in Scripted mode by a fixed schedule of bursts.
  • Draw every random value (driver type, passing) at the moment of arrival, from the lane's own System.Random.
  • Place the car when the spawn point is clear; otherwise hold it in a queue, never drop it.
  • Configure the new CarAgent and register it with the lane's light.
  • Remove the car once it has driven far enough or finished its path.

02A random stream per lane

Unity's global Random would interleave draws between the seven spawners in an order set by frame timing. IntersectionConfig.ResetAllLanes seeds each spawner from the run seed plus a hash of the lane name, so lane B's cars are the same whatever happens in lane A.

03Arrivals: Poisson or scripted

A Poisson process has exponentially distributed gaps: gap = −ln(1 − u) × mean, where u is uniform on 0-1. A batch always uses Poisson; the control panel's scripted cases use schedules, so a scripted run is identical every time.

What if: change the arrival rate or the driver mix

The gap formula and the driver roll are the code's. The random numbers here come from a small generator in the page, not from .NET's System.Random, so the dots show the rule, not the cars of any particular run.

04Never drop a car

When a queue backs up to the spawn point, a new car cannot be placed. Discarding it would quietly cut demand exactly when traffic is heaviest, and make both controllers look better than they are. So arrivals wait in pending, and every random value is drawn on arrival, never on release:

The counter delayedArrivals records how often this happened. Under the old ABACEvent rates, queues reached the spawn points and the spawners could release nothing further; ten runs of batch v3 gridlocked that way (SODA_BASELINE §8.16).
