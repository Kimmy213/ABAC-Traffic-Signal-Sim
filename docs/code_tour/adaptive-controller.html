01Big picture

SODA (Sampled-Observation Demand-Adaptive) is the controller the project proposes. It may use only what a camera without a tracker can give it: a count of vehicles in each detection zone, once a second. From that it decides which lanes get green next and for how long. Everything it remembers between readings is eight numbers: two per signal head.

  • Watch: every snapshotInterval (1 s), read the four heads' counts and note when each head went from empty to occupied (queueStart).
  • Decide: when there is demand, answer four questions: is anyone starving (Q1), who queued first (Q2), who can go with them (Q3), and how long (Q4).
  • Act: turn lights yellow, red and green through TrafficLight.SetState, then end the green early on gap-out, anti-starvation or max green.
  • Report: hand every phase to DataLogger and keep the counters the summary CSV prints.

02Signal heads: the unit of control

SODA does not control lanes; it controls lamps. A2 and A deliberately share one TrafficLight object, so the junction's five signalised lanes collapse into four heads. Each head carries only the two numbers below, plus the lists it was built from.

BuildHeads runs in Awake. It walks the signalised lanes from IntersectionConfig and puts lanes that point at the same light into one head. It also collects each head's zones and gives it a priority from the junction's original phase order:

03Eyes: the snapshot

A head's count is the sum of GetSampledCount() over its zones. That is the one ROIZone method a tracker-free camera can supply; a test (SodaTest) checks that this file never calls the three that need a tracker.

One clock. Every decision waits for snapshotIndex to change (WaitForNextSnapshot), so nothing reads the zones faster or slower than the data arrives. The only other timer is the COLLECT ceiling, a timeout that looks at no data.

Because all four heads are stamped inside one Snapshot() call, two heads whose first cars arrive in the same second get exactly the same queueStart. Ties are routine, which is why ORIGINAL_SEQUENCE exists. In batch v5 the tie-break ran in 812 (Morning), 388 (Midday), 2,041 (Evening) and 1,878 (ABACEvent) phases, summed over 27 runs. Bx was the head left out in 305, 60, 665 and 596 of them (TieBreaksApplied and TieBreaksExcludingBx in summary_all_runs.csv; the counters also include the 120 s warm-up).

04The round: ALL-RED, COLLECT, SERVE

RunSoda is the main loop, started in Start and again by RestartCycle. The real signal goes all-red when the junction is empty, and so does SODA; it does not rest in green.

COLLECT is the only place demand is allowed to build up before a decision. The 7 s is a ceiling; the code leaves early when two heads have cars, or when any head has three:

Under heavy traffic the junction never empties, so after the first COLLECT, SERVE simply repeats. In batch v5, COLLECT ran only once per run (27 times in 27 runs) in EveningPeak and ABACEvent, 65 times in MorningPeak and 108 times in MiddayNormal (CollectCount).

05SERVE: one phase, four questions

ServeOnePhase runs once per phase. It takes a fresh reading at the moment of decision, then closes out the phase that just ended. That reset must come before the questions; the comment explains what went wrong when it came after.

Q1 starvation and Q2 leader

Q1 and Q2 both rank heads with one comparison: oldest queueStart first, and on an exact tie the original order A → B → Bx → CB. Time decides who goes. The vehicle count is not consulted here; the comment on ChooseLeaderByQueueStart explains that ranking by count would let H_A win every time and Bx would never get green.

Q3 the group

The leader seeds a group. Every other head with at least minDemandToJoinGroup cars is tried in the same oldest-first order and joins if it conflicts with nobody already in the group. A2 is then forced in beside A, because it is the same lamp. Heads with no cars are skipped for the whole phase; the comment calls this the single largest source of the win over Fixed.

Clearance is paid only by lanes leaving the group. In S4 phase 2, B stays green from phase 1 into phase 2 while A goes yellow.

Try it: Q1 to Q4 on your own numbers

Set the count in each zone and when each head's queue began, then read the decision. The rules are a line-for-line port of the methods above, run with the code's constants. The four buttons load the decision points of traces S2 and S4.

06Q4: how long a green

G is computed after the clearance, at the moment green opens, from a fresh count. Each lane in the group needs startupLostTime + D × saturationHeadway seconds to discharge D cars. The lanes run in parallel, so the phase is sized by the longest need, then held between gMin and gMax.

What if: change the green-time constants

Both constants were measured by Itim from 39 headways on lane B, morning footage of 14 July 2026: 2.95 s startup lost time and 2.31 s per car (sd 0.81). The textbook HCM values are 2.0 and 2.0 (SODA_BASELINE §4.4). The thin grey line stays at the code's values for comparison.

Check against the baseline's response table: D = 4 gives 12.19 s, D = 10 gives 26.05 s, and from D = 19 every phase gets 45 s.

What if: gap-out sensitivity

Click a reading to change it. Gap-out needs the whole group to read zero on consecutive snapshots. SodaTest §2 checks that readings 3, 0, 2 do not fire and 3, 0, 0 do. Because the rule counts readings, moving snapshotInterval from 2 s to 1 s on 14 Sep also halved the confirmation window from 4 s to 2 s. Itim accepted that knowingly.

What if: the anti-starvation floor

In EveningPeak and ABACEvent every lane is waiting, so some head always crosses 40.5 s. The cut then fires the moment it is allowed to. In batch v5, 98% of those starvation cuts came within 21 s of green opening, so the 20 s floor, not the formula, set the length of almost every green in those two scenarios. That is why SODA does not beat Fixed there (Evening +6.3% wait, p = 0.08). Move the sliders to see when a cut lands.

A comparison of 20, 30 and 40 s floors has not been run, so the effect of changing it on the wait is unknown.

08The reset that keeps it fair

When a head's green ends and it still has cars, its queueStart restarts at now. Without that, a head that never empties would keep its original timestamp, win Q2 forever and lock every other head out. The tests show it: in a 10-phase run with no head ever emptying, the four heads were served 3, 2, 3 and 2 times; with the reset removed, Bx took all 10 (SodaTest §1).

Consequence for how SODA is described. Under saturation, queueStart comes from the reset rule, not from when cars arrived. The service order is then round-robin weighted by demand, not first-come first-served (SODA_BASELINE §3.4, limitation L2). In v5 the reset fired 3,219 to 3,372 times per 27 runs in the three busy scenarios (QueueStartResets).

The two measured constants are const, so the scene cannot overwrite them. The public fields that use them can be overwritten, so Awake checks them first:

10Records and the Decision Inspector

At the end of every green, LogPhaseTrace sends one row to DataLogger: the group, G given, time used, why it ended, and when each lane first read empty. The last of these exposes idle green, a lane held green with nothing left to serve (trace S5).

ForceStop is how ScenarioManager and the control panel switch controllers. Since 11 Sep it also clears the SODA counters. Before that, 107 of 108 Fixed rows in a batch carried the previous Adaptive run's values.

The Decision Inspector (press D in Play mode) draws the queue and Q1-Q4 for the latest phase, filled in by TraceDecisionInputs, TraceOption and TraceVerdict. It changes no state. ScenarioManager switches it off during a batch.

12Known issues and decisions

  • Fixed 11 SepHeads that stayed in the group kept a stale queueStart and won Q1 and Q2 indefinitely. In EveningPeak Rep17, H_B stayed in the group for 24 phases (365.5 s), H_CB was never served and CB cars waited up to 344 s. The reset now covers continuing heads too.Comment above line 714; QueueClockTest; SODA_BASELINE §8.7
  • Fixed 14 SepThe end-of-phase reset ran after Q1/Q2, so a head just served could win Q2 again, or trigger a starvation override for itself. Moved before the questions, matching soda.py. 30 of 11,670 phases in batch v2 had a green long enough to hit this.Comment at lines 627-637
  • Fixed 10 SepFindStarvingHead used >=, which made the last head win a tie, the opposite of Q2 and of soda.py. It now uses CompareHeads.SODA_BASELINE §8.3; SodaTest §9
  • Fixed 11 SepForceStop did not clear the counters, and the phase number stayed at 0 while the panel was hidden.SODA_BASELINE §8.8
  • Decision 14 SepsnapshotInterval 2 s → 1 s (Itim). The gap-out window halves to 2 s as a known consequence.Comment at lines 35-38
  • RemovedThe counting window: it asked COLLECT's question a second time, so one vehicle waited 11 s. Rest-in-green and per-vehicle tracking were also removed; the real signal has neither.SODA_BASELINE §6
  • OpenIdle green: a lane that empties early stays green until the whole group is empty (S5: 16.05 s for {B = 10, Bx = 3}). A per-lane gap-out was agreed as future work.SODA_BASELINE §8.4, limitation L6
  • OpenOne starvation episode is counted twice (the cut and the override). Divide StarvationEvents by two when reporting episodes.Limitation L13
  • OpenLoadMeasuredPreset is a context-menu preset without EditorUtility.SetDirty, so Save may not write it. ScenarioManager's presets were fixed; this one is in the signal system and has been left alone.Comment on ScenarioManager.MarkDirty
  • OpenTests/soda.py in this repository still samples at 2 s.SODA_BASELINE §8.13
